
**2 major types of photoreceptors**

**Rods**
- Responsible for black and white vision
- Highly sensitive to dim light
- Important for night vision (scotopic vision)
- Do not mediate color vision
- Most numerous in peripheral retina

**Cones**
- Responsible for color vision
- Important for detailed and acute vision
- Function best in bright light (photopic vision)
- Concentrated in central retina, esp. fovea

**General Visual Pathway:**
Light → Rods and cones → Retinal neurons → Ganglion cells → Optic nerve fibers → Brain → Visual cortex

**Functional Segments of Rods and Cones:**
1. Outer Segment
- Contains the light-sensitive photochemical pigments.
- Rods contain **rhodopsin**.
- Cones contain **color pigments**.
- Contains numerous membrane discs.

2. Inner Segment
- Contains cytoplasm and cellular organelles.
- Rich in mitochondria.
- Mitochondria provide energy required for photoreceptor function.

3. Nucleus
- Contains genetic material controlling cellular activities

4. Synaptic Body
- Connects photoreceptors to subsequent retinal neurons
- Forms synapses with bipolar and horizontal cells


**Membranous Discs**
- Each disc is an infolded shelf of cell membrane.
- A rod or cone may contain approximately 1,000 discs.
- Rhodopsin and cone pigments are incorporated into disc membranes as transmembrane proteins.
- Photosensitive pigments account for approximately 40% of the mass of the outer segment.
