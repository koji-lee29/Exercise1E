#anatomy

- Light is composed of different wavelengths.
- Different wavelengths stimulate the three cone types to different degrees.
- Each cone type responds to a range of wavelengths, but their sensitivities differ.
- The nervous system compares the relative stimulation of the red-, green-, and blue-sensitive cones.
- The combined signals are interpreted by the brain as different colors.

**Trichromatic Theory of Color Vision:**

- Normal color vision depends on the combined activity of three types of cones.
- Different combinations and degrees of stimulation produce different color sensations.
- Approximately equal stimulation of all three cone types can produce the perception of white light.
- If one cone type is missing or abnormal, the ability to distinguish certain colors becomes impaired.




**Humans can perceive a wide range of colors despite having only three types of cone cells because each cone type responds to different wavelengths of light. These three types are sensitive to short (blue), medium (green), and long (red) wavelengths. When light enters the eye, the cone cells are stimulated in different combinations and degrees depending on the wavelength. The brain processes and compares these signals, allowing us to distinguish millions of different colors and shades. Therefore, color vision depends not only on the three types of cones but also on how the brain interprets their combined signals.**
